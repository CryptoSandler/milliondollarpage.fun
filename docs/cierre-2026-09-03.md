## 1 · The vertical register keeps the fix, and the switch is gone

The parade down the two letterboxes had a bug the owner compared side by side on
a local server before choosing: a bare item's size label is centred inside its
own box, and on a narrow item — a 6×28 comes out 34px wide at the height cap —
the label is **wider than the box it is centred in**, so it escaped and painted
over the artwork of the item below it.

| | items with a label outside its box | worst spill |
|---|---|---|
| before (`?ticker=v1`) | **24 of 80** | 29px |
| after (`?ticker=v2`) | **0 of 80** | — |

Measured at 2495×1484 against the twenty-purchase fixture. Nothing else
differed: no item was being squashed in either version, and both columns already
started flush with the frame at y=34 — the gap the owner saw at the top of the
left column was Next's own dev-mode badge, not the site.

`?ticker=v2` won. The rules are unconditional now and **the query parameter, the
`data-ticker-v` attribute and the v1 branch are deleted** — a switch left in
after a decision is a second layout nobody is testing.

**One thing was touched in the register beyond deleting v1, and it is flagged
here because the instruction was not to.** The wall is empty in production, so
the column shows its empty state — and `Nothing sold yet` is a single `nowrap`
line about 100px wide inside a column that is **101px** at 1440 with 10px of
padding on each side. It would have shipped as `Nothing sold ye`, clipped by the
scroller, on the wall's first day and in the state the owner asked to have
verified. The change is four declarations scoped to
`[data-ticker="sides"] .board-tape__empty`: wrap, centre, balance. It cannot
reach the wall either way — the scroller is `overflow: hidden` and the column is
exactly the letterbox wide. **Revert is one block in `globals.css`** if the
owner would rather have had the report than the fix.

## 3 · The header and the strip, three zones each

Both bands are `1fr auto 1fr` grids rather than flex rows.

**Header.** Wordmark left. The figure centred — `712,872` in 22px mono, `PIXELS
LEFT` under it, `· 28.71% sold` as a small suffix instead of a competing pill.
Right, in the order a reader reaches: *What this is*, *How to buy*, the theme
switch, **Connect wallet** hard against the edge.

| | counter's left edge, 2495 window | centred? |
|---|---|---|
| before | x=1617 | no — a flex row centres on what the sides leave over, and the wallet control changes width when it holds an address |
| after | x=1115, width 271, centre **1250** | yes — window centre is 1247 |

**`1 online · N views` has left the bar.** The count is on `/stats` with its
history. The element that BEATS — the one that tells the server this browser is
here — moved into the register, which is the one piece of chrome that exists in
every layout, so presence never has a width at which it has nowhere to be.

**Strip.** Presets and zoom left, purchase panel centred, nothing else. The
panel is **560px** wide, down from the 980 it took at 2495 when it was handed
the strip's slack; inside it the readout is left, Buy is right, and the red
refusal is **on its own line under both** — which is what stops a long sentence
setting the panel's width.

| viewport | strip height, before → after | panel width, before → after | panel centred on the window |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2495×1484 | 93 → **93** | 980 → **560** | yes (968…1528, centre 1248) |
| 1440×900 | 93 → **93** | 882 → **560** | no — see the arithmetic below |
| 1280×800 | 93 → **93** | — → **560** | no |
| 390×844 | 221 → **221** | — → full width | n/a, one column |

**Where the centring gives way, and why that is the right thing to lose.**
Centring a 560px panel needs `(V − 560) / 2 ≥ 534`, because the tools are 510
wide and the strip's padding and gap are the rest. That holds from **1920 up**.
At 1440 and 1280 the panel sits to the right of the tools instead — still 560,
still not truncating anything. The alternative is a panel narrow enough to
centre at 1440, which the same arithmetic puts at **372px**, and at 372 the
readout the panel exists to show is clipped.

**One trap found by measuring rather than by looking.** A `1fr` grid track's
automatic minimum is its item's *min-content*, and the preset pill wraps by
design (so it cannot paint off the side of a phone). The first grid squeezed the
tools to 336px at 1280, they took two rows, and the strip grew from 93 to
**123** — 30px of wall lost at the one viewport with the least of it. `min-width:
max-content` on the tools above 640px is the fix.

## 4 · The chrome budget is 127, and it is a measurement

**≤127px without rails** (34px header + 93px strip), **≤34px with rails**,
**≤256px at 390**. Written into `DESIGN.md` with the date and the reason, and
`scripts/board-share.mts` enforces exactly it — no slack, so the next thing that
grows the chrome has to say so out loud.

**It is not the ~98 the owner estimated, and the arithmetic says why.** The
strip's height is the purchase panel's, and the panel is a 46px readout over a
refusal that is allowed **two lines**. Clipping that refusal to one line buys
14px of wall and costs a buyer the reason their Buy button is dead. That trade
is not taken. The rule that outranks the number is still *nothing stands on the
wall, at any width* — the old ≤60 idle belonged to the regime where the preset
pill stood ON the board between selections, and it does not survive that rule.

**And the wall's share did not move.** `board-share.mts`, after the reorder,
against the same table `DESIGN.md` recorded on 2026-09-02:

| viewport | chrome | budget | board | share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1440×900 | 127px | 127px | 1181×757 | 69.0% |
| 1920×1080 | 127px | 127px | 1462×937 | 66.1% |
| 2495×1484 | 127px | 127px | 2093×1341 | 75.8% |
| 2560×1440 | 127px | 127px | 2024×1297 | 71.2% |
| 1280×800 | 127px | 127px | 1024×657 | 65.7% |
| 390×844 | 256px | 256px | 374×241 | 27.4% |
| 3440×1440 (rails) | 34px | 34px | 2170×1390 | 60.9% |

Every percentage is identical to the one before the reorder, idle and with the
panel open measure the same, **nothing overlaps the board's own box at any row**,
and the join at 2495 is exact: `24.1 + 156.5 = 180.7px` against an item 180.7px
tall.

## What is left, and both are the owner's

### The mailbox

`contact@milliondollarpage.fun` is printed on eight surfaces and **nothing
receives it yet**. It is deliberately not a link: a `mailto:` on an address that
bounces spends somebody's message without telling them it failed.

Namecheap → Domain List → `milliondollarpage.fun` → **Mail Settings**, and there
are two roads:

- **Email Forwarding**, free, on the Domain tab: add `contact` → the
  CryptoSandler Gmail. Mail arrives immediately; replies still leave from the
  Gmail address.
- **Private Email**, which is what the owner chose for the end of the build: buy
  the plan, *Set Up Mail Service* (it writes its own MX records), create the
  `contact` mailbox in the Private Email dashboard. Replies then come **from**
  this address.

**When it is live, the change here is one line in two files:** wrap the address
in an `<a href="mailto:…">` in `src/components/SiteFooter.tsx` and in the FAQ
answer, and delete the `mailto:` assertion in
`src/app/__tests__/contact.test.tsx`. Nothing else moves.

### The noindex

The site is closed to crawlers by **three locks**, and lifting them is a
three-file change, all three or none — two out of three is a site that is
indexed anyway and looks protected:

1. `src/app/robots.ts` — `Disallow: /`, advisory and read before the fetch;
2. `metadata.robots` in `src/app/layout.tsx` — the `<meta>` tag, which is the
   only one that governs a URL somebody has already been sent;
3. `X-Robots-Tag: noindex, nofollow` on `/:path*` in `next.config.ts` — for the
   responses with no `<head>`: the API routes, the wall PNG, the RSS feed.

`src/app/__tests__/noindex.test.ts` asserts all three together and will go red
the moment one is lifted alone. **Nobody but the owner decides when.** The
decision is not technical — the wall has nothing on it, and the first thing a
search engine would index is an empty board.
